Abstract

Emotional expression is crucial in Text-to-Speech systems as it enhances the naturalness, expressiveness, and empathy of synthesized speech. Despite advances in TTS technology, current systems face challenges in modeling emotional expression due to the limitations of discrete emotional categories and continuous dimensional models, which struggle to capture the complexity of over 34,000 human emotional states and the “one-to-many” mapping problem. To address these issues, we propose SGEANet, a framework that bridges the gap between discrete emotion categories and continuous emotion representations, enabling accurate and controllable emotional expression in speech synthesis. SGEANet uses emotion basis vectors to model emotional expressions, incorporating dynamic interactions between emotions. We introduce an emotion anchor regression learning strategy that uses emotion categories as supervision, ensuring better alignment and discriminability between emotion vectors and their corresponding acoustic patterns. Experimental results show that SGEANet outperforms existing methods in both subjective and objective evaluations, demonstrating its ability to precisely control emotional expression and synthesize rich, mixed emotions like “bittersweet” with high naturalness and expressiveness.
